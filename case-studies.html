Case 01

Customer Journey · Digital Operations · Business Systems

Music School

Digital Operations, Enrollment & Customer Journey Transformation

The project started with a request to find school-management software that could reduce administrative work.

After reviewing the operation, I found a more immediate issue. The school first needed to improve how families discovered, understood and enrolled in its programs.

01

What was happening

The administrative problem was real, but it was not the first problem to solve.

Families had to understand programs, age groups, levels, locations, teachers, schedules, tuition, trial options and enrollment steps before they could make a decision.

That information was spread across pages and tools. Schedules were difficult to maintain. Trial and enrollment paths were disconnected. Pricing and registration were not always easy to understand.

02

What I found

Implementing new software first would not solve the immediate revenue problem.

The more urgent constraint was the customer journey. Parents needed a clearer path from interest to program selection, trial registration and enrollment.

That changed the order of the project.

03

What changed

Phase one focused on enrollment and revenue before back-office transformation.

  • Restructured program pages around how parents compare classes.
  • Improved schedules, filters and calendar presentation.
  • Clarified tuition, trials and enrollment options.
  • Improved registration and checkout paths.
  • Created email, SEO, social and enrollment marketing assets.

Phase two

I translated real school workflows into system requirements, researched available platforms and shortlisted five systems for hands-on testing.

Testing covered family accounts, sibling discounts, multi-location scheduling, trial-to-enrollment workflows, payments, attendance, staff communication and parent portals.

Business impact

The first change was not software. It was priority.

~4× first-month registrations and revenue compared with the same period the previous year
Same programs and the same enrollment timing as the comparison period
5 systems shortlisted for hands-on workflow testing

Case 02

Business Systems · Operations · Process Transformation

Staffing Agency

Business Operations & Recruiting Systems Transformation

The agency needed to grow beyond a highly manual operating model.

Candidate acquisition was difficult, the existing ATS was limiting database reuse, and there was no scalable marketing or reporting system.

01

What was happening

The business was trying to scale with tools and workflows built for a much smaller operation.

Candidate sourcing took hours. Resume storage was limited. Database search was weak. Duplicate profiles and rejected candidates were difficult to manage.

Despite ongoing promotion, candidate volume was roughly 300 resumes per month with a similar number of active jobs.

02

What I found

This was not only an ATS problem.

Candidate acquisition, database reuse, reporting, website traffic, referral channels and recruiter workflows were all connected.

Replacing one tool without redesigning the surrounding operation would only move the bottleneck.

03

What changed

I rebuilt the operating ecosystem around acquisition, search, reporting and adoption.

  • Compared ATS and workforce platforms across more than 90 criteria.
  • Tested recruiter workflows before selecting the platform.
  • Configured ATS, CRM, workforce, onboarding, permissions, pipelines and integrations.
  • Added free job-board integrations, referral workflows, website applications and social acquisition.
  • Improved candidate data structure and Boolean search.
  • Built reporting around sources, bottlenecks, costs, channel performance and hiring feasibility.
  • Created training, written guidance and ongoing adoption support.

Business impact

A searchable, reusable recruiting operation replaced a mostly reactive workflow.

~1.5× resume flow without active promotion
2–3× resume flow with limited promotion
20K+ searchable candidate database
Hours → minutes for selected sourcing workflows

Case 03

Digital Transformation · Automation · Event Operations

Online CME Systems

Digital Operations & Automation Consultant

Healthcare education programs needed to move online without becoming dependent on an expensive all-in-one platform.

I built the operating system myself using a low-cost mix of websites, forms, payments, automation and data tools.

01

What was happening

Physical events could serve hundreds of people. The new environment needed to serve thousands.

Registration, communication, payments, access, attendance, testing and certification were separate tasks.

Moving each task online separately would have created a digital version of the same manual process.

02

What I found

The goal was not to move an event online. It was to redesign the entire participant lifecycle.

Promotion, registration, payment, access, live delivery, testing, certification, recordings, follow-up and reporting needed to operate as one connected workflow.

03

What changed

I built an end-to-end CME delivery system using low-cost tools.

  • Built more than 50 multilingual landing pages.
  • Connected online registration and payment workflows.
  • Automated participant communication and access.
  • Supported live broadcasts for large online audiences.
  • Connected testing and certificate generation.
  • Created recording access and follow-up workflows.
  • Maintained participant data for reporting and auditability.

Business impact

The operation moved from location-limited events to a scalable digital model.

1K–4K participants at one time
100–500 typical participant range before the online model
4K → 45K+ healthcare professional database growth

Case 04

Event Operations · Digital Transformation

Healthcare Marketing & CME Agency

CME Event Project Manager

Before the larger online transformation, the event operation still depended heavily on physical outreach and paper registration.

01

What was happening

Audience growth depended on people physically delivering invitations.

Staff visited clinics, distributed invitations and later re-entered information from handwritten registration forms.

The process took time, limited geographic reach and introduced errors into the participant database.

02

What I found

Registration was not only an event-day task. It was part of audience acquisition and data quality.

Moving registration online could improve both reach and the accuracy of the information used after the event.

03

What changed

  • Introduced event landing pages.
  • Added online registration.
  • Expanded digital promotion.
  • Reduced paper registration and repeated manual data entry.
  • Created more structured participant-data workflows.

Business impact

Event acquisition began moving from physical outreach to a reusable digital audience.

Manual → digital promotion, registration and participant data
Wider reach beyond clinic-by-clinic invitations
80–100 events managed annually

Case 05

Operating Model · Customer Behavior · Service Design

Hotel & Restaurant Complex

Banquet & Event Manager · Operating Model Redesign

Internal event services were selling poorly. The obvious response would have been to push the team to sell more.

The real problem was how the service model was designed.

01

What was happening

Clients spoke with different managers at different stages of the same event.

The banquet discussion came first. Additional event services were introduced later, when clients were already tired from making decisions.

Staff schedules also did not match customer behavior. Many customers preferred to discuss events after work or on weekends.

02

What I found

Low sales were a symptom of fragmented ownership, decision fatigue and poor coverage.

The operating model expected customers to adapt to the business. It needed to work the other way around.

03

What changed

  • Combined banquet and event-service responsibilities.
  • Created one primary point of contact through the planning process.
  • Introduced a 3-days-on, 3-days-off rotation.
  • Expanded evening and weekend availability.
  • Aligned commissions with both banquet and event-service revenue.

Business impact

Redesigning the operating model changed both customer behavior and revenue.

~90% of banquet events used internal event services
~4–6× monthly vendor-fee revenue
Better coverage evenings and weekends without relying on constant informal overtime
