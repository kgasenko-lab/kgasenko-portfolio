Music School

Enrollment & Operations Transformation

The project began as a search for school-management software. The evidence showed that the more urgent constraint was earlier in the journey: families could not easily understand, compare and enroll in programs.

Why the software request was not the first priority

Enrollment, scheduling, payments, communication and student updates were consuming too much administrative time, so replacing the management platform looked like the logical first step. But reviewing the customer journey revealed a different bottleneck. Program information, age groups, schedules, tuition, trial options and registration paths were difficult for families to compare—and difficult for the school to maintain consistently.

Implementing a new platform at that moment would have reorganized the back office without solving the immediate enrollment and revenue problem. I changed the sequence of the work: improve acquisition first, then select the system against requirements proven through the redesigned operation.

I rebuilt program pages, schedules, pricing, trial and enrollment paths, email assets, SEO foundations and campaign materials. After the customer journey was clearer, I mapped the school’s operational requirements, reviewed available platforms and shortlisted five systems for hands-on testing.

Measured outcome~4×

First-month registrations and revenue compared with the same programs and enrollment timing in the prior year.

Featured case · Business systems · Recruiting operations

TBest Staffing Agency

Candidate Acquisition & ATS Operating-System Reset

The business needed more candidates and clearer recruiting performance. The request looked like an ATS replacement; the real work was rebuilding the operating system around it.

From an ATS problem to an operating-system reset

The existing process could produce up to approximately 300 resumes a month, but only with continuous promotion, and that volume was not enough for bulk hiring. The website generated almost no organic resume flow. Inside the ATS, limited Boolean search, weak parsing and storage, poor integrations and inconsistent duplicate and do-not-rehire tracking made the candidate database difficult to reuse.

Replacing the ATS alone would have moved the same bottleneck into a different platform. Candidate acquisition, referrals, website visibility, recruiter workflows, database quality, reporting and user adoption were all connected. I treated them as one operating ecosystem rather than a software-purchasing exercise.

I compared platforms across more than 90 criteria, tested real recruiter workflows, implemented a low-cost ATS/CRM/workforce stack, configured the processes, trained users and built reporting that gave leadership a clearer view of sources, pipeline activity and recruiting performance.

Measured outcome2–3×

Candidate flow with limited promotion; approximately 1.5× without active promotion.

20K+

Searchable candidate records, with selected searches reduced from hours to minutes.

Digital transformation · Automation · Event operations

Online CME Systems

End-to-End Digital Delivery System

Healthcare education programs had to move online and support thousands of participants without becoming dependent on an expensive all-in-one platform.

Building the operation behind the online event

The visible goal was to move physical CME events online. Yet registration, payment, participant communication, access, attendance, testing and certification were still handled as separate tasks. Digitizing each manual step independently would have recreated the same fragmented operation on a screen.

I designed the full participant lifecycle as one connected delivery system and built it myself with a near-zero-cost technology stack. More than 50 multilingual landing pages connected promotion and registration with payments, event access, live delivery, attendance, testing, certification, recordings, participant data, reporting and follow-up.

The system expanded capacity while reducing manual work and dependence on third-party providers. It also turned one-time event records into a professional audience database that could support future programs.

Delivery capacity1K–4K

Participants supported at one time, compared with roughly 100–500 in physical programs.

4K → 45K+

Growth of the professional participant database.

Event operations · Digital acquisition

Healthcare Marketing & CME Agency

Audience Acquisition & Registration Transformation

Event growth depended on clinic visits, paper invitations and handwritten registration. Improving attendance meant redesigning acquisition and data capture together.

Turning registration into a reusable acquisition system

Staff visited clinics, delivered physical invitations and followed up manually. At the event, handwritten registration required repeated data entry and introduced errors in names, email addresses and participant records. The process limited reach before the event and reduced the quality of the data available afterward.

I reframed registration as part of audience acquisition—not simply an event-day administrative task. Event landing pages, online registration, digital promotion and structured participant-data workflows created a continuous path from first contact to attendance and follow-up.

The organization could promote beyond one clinic at a time, maintain cleaner participant information and reuse its audience across a large annual event calendar rather than rebuilding reach for every program.

Operating scale80–100

Events supported annually through a reusable digital audience and structured data workflow.

Manual → digital

Promotion, registration and participant-data capture.

Operating model · Customer behavior · Service design

Hotel & Restaurant Complex

Service & Operating Model Redesign

Internal event services were selling poorly. The apparent sales problem was actually a mismatch between client behavior, employee coverage and ownership of the relationship.

Redesigning the service around how clients made decisions

Banquet services and additional event services were handled by different managers working limited standard schedules. Clients, however, often wanted to discuss decisions during evenings and weekends. Handoffs arrived late in the buying process, added decision fatigue and allowed revenue to leave the business.

The issue was not that managers needed to “sell harder.” The operating model expected customers to adapt to the company’s internal structure. I combined banquet and event-service responsibilities so one manager could own the relationship across the journey, aligned commissions with the complete sale and introduced two-manager coverage on a three-days-on, three-days-off rotation.

The redesigned model matched coverage to customer behavior while creating stronger earning opportunities and more sustainable rest for managers.

Of banquet events adopted internal event services.

~4–6×

Increase in monthly vendor-fee revenue.
